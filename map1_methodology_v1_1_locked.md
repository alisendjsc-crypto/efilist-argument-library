## Q1 DECISION — Sophisticate tier-elevation: **APPROVED as written**
Sophisticate mode boosts tier-elevating edges (T1→T4) and suppresses tier-regressing edges (T4→T1). Formal philosophers escalate rather than regress. No modification.

## Q2 DECISION — Defender collapse (T4→T1): **APPROVED as written**
Defender mode boosts T4→T1 collapse edges. A defender losing a philosophical argument characteristically regresses to ad hominem. User note: drifter behavior may appear similar in form, but the *generative mechanism* is different (collapse = pressure-driven; drift = energy-driven). Both modes can produce overlapping T4→T1 edges; convergence between them is meaningful signal, not redundancy.

## Q3 DECISION — Nuclear retreat (T1→T5): **Present in BOTH Defender and Drifter modes**
Nuclear retreat appears in Defender mode (worldview-defense-driven) AND Drifter mode (emotional-exhaustion-driven). The two generative mechanisms produce the same edge for different reasons. Display treatment: add a **user-facing disclosure note** on every nuclear-retreat edge and every collapse edge that surfaces the two-mechanism distinction transparently. Not an admin log — a user-readable explanation that appears on edge hover/click.

Disclosure template (will appear verbatim in edge tooltips):
> **Two-mechanism edge.** This move shows up in both Defender and Drifter modes because two different forces can produce it. The Defender reading: the interlocutor feels their worldview under existential threat and reaches for the most extreme accusation available. The Drifter reading: the interlocutor is emotionally depleted and grabbing the highest-charge rhetorical weapon within reach. Same move, different fuel. In practice you may not be able to tell which is driving them — and it may not matter. The counter is the same either way.

## Q4 DECISION — Disengagement annotation: **APPROVED**
Add per-node `disengagement_probability` field (LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH). Surfaces how often the objection dead-ends without a successor. Rendered as a small badge on the node in Map 1.

Heuristic for setting it:
- **HIGH disengagement** (interlocutor often just stops): nodes where the response is a clean philosophical checkmate with no satisfying next move for the objector — `performative-contradiction`, `consent-incoherent` (when defeated), `bradley-no-subject`, `non-identity-problem`.
- **MEDIUM disengagement**: most T4/T5 nodes — the debate either continues or the interlocutor disengages, roughly balanced.
- **LOW disengagement** (interlocutor almost always continues): T1/T2 nodes driven by TMT or Optimism Bias — the emotional investment keeps them talking. `life-gift`, `just-depressed`, `most-people-happy`, `gods-plan`.

## Q5 DECISION — Canonical sophisticate overrides: **Generate overrides now, log transparently**
Rather than flag-and-wait, I'll encode canonical sophisticate successors for the ~15 T4/T5 high-philosophy nodes based on philosophical convention — the textbook next move that any trained ethicist would make. Each override edge is marked `canonical_override: true` in the data so it's transparently distinguishable from rule-generated edges. The in-tool disclosure note on each override:

> **Canonical override.** This edge wasn't generated by the premise-matching algorithm; it was added because the move is a recognized standard sequence in academic philosophy (e.g., any attack on Benatar's asymmetry naturally routes to Boonin's formal critique, regardless of premise overlap). These overrides were added by the generator without independent expert validation — if you spot one that seems wrong, it's a candidate for correction in the next methodology pass.

## Q6 DECISION — Convergence weighting: **Separate convergence tier, displayed transparently**
Mode-local weights are preserved (Sophisticate mode's LOW edge stays LOW in its own view). Convergence adds a *separate indicator* that does not override the mode-local weight. In Blended view, edges show:
- **Mode-local weight**: inherited per-mode (HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW)
- **Convergence tier**: ★★★ (all 3 modes agree) / ★★ (2 modes agree) / ★ (single mode) — rendered as small star indicator next to the edge
- **Math panel** on edge click: shows the per-mode scores side by side, with the aggregation visible

This preserves the "three modes disagreeing is signal, not noise" principle. A ★ edge with HIGH mode-local weight is *still* HIGH — it just reflects a fork that only one interlocutor type would take. A ★★★ edge with MEDIUM mode-local weights is a reliable prediction even though no single mode rates it HIGH.

Three disclosure elements need to appear in the user interface, each designed to be discrete (not jarring) but transparent (surfaces the reasoning):

All three are opt-in: user sees a small icon indicating the note exists; clicking expands it. Not modal, not jarring. Same interaction pattern as the review-confidence flags on Map 3.
