# self-effacing-under-universalization — LONG

This objection concedes what every other one contests — grant that procreation is impermissible — and attacks the rule's form instead: universalized, "do not procreate" extinguishes the community of agents who could hold, transmit, or be bound by it, so it cannot serve as universal moral legislation whatever the empty world is worth. Take the concession seriously; the charge still fails the Kantian test it invokes. Universalized false-promising is unthinkable because the maxim parasitizes the very practice it destroys. Universal non-procreation parasitizes nothing: a last generation keeping its rule to the end is fully conceivable — there is no contradiction in conception. And structure alone overgenerates. Any morality that merely permits universal childlessness — every view lacking a procreative duty, which is nearly all of them — universalizes to the same quiet terminus. To bite antinatalism specifically, the objector must either assert a duty to procreate, which conscripts new persons into existence as means to the moral order's continuation — the instrumentalization Kant's own Humanity formula forbids — or retreat to the badness of the empty world, the axiological argument this objection advertised itself as not needing. Nor does "legislation" fail: unheld is not unholdable. While the rule has addressees, every one of them can coherently adopt it; afterward it binds vacuously, like any rule whose scope has emptied. Universal compliance is a norm's success condition, not its breakdown — eradication protocols retire themselves without incoherence, and a fully public "do not procreate" is advanced, not undermined, by universal avowal; the publicity condition is satisfied in precisely the way esoteric moralities fail it. Parfit named the remainder: a self-effacing principle is not thereby self-defeating; its practical fate among its holders is a fact about holders, not a defect in the principle. What survives is the contradiction-in-the-will — can a rational agent will that rational agency not continue? The maxim ends no existing agent. It declines to manufacture new ones, and the formulas protect persons, not the headcount of a type. The objector who insists that rational nature's continuation is itself an unconditional end has stopped making a formal point and started making an axiological one — a claim about the value of future existence, on ground this library has already mapped and bitten. The structural charge is defeated here. The retreat exits to the asymmetry cluster, where it was already awaited.
