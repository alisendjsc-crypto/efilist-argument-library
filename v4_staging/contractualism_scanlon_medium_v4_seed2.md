The strongest contractualist case is ex-ante: principles are justified by the prospects they offer, so a principle permitting procreation under good-enough conditions offers the created a prospect no rational agent could reasonably reject — hypothetical consent being the same currency that licenses surgery on the unconscious, parental authority, and social risk. Three failures, each internal to the framework. The standpoint has no occupant: every legitimating analogy justifies risk to someone who already exists; creation manufactures its own justificand, so before the decision no one faces the prospect, and afterward someone faces only the fait accompli. The token's veto is laundered: Scanlon's test is justifiability to each, not to the average; the child who draws the catastrophic life is told her complaint was priced into a standpoint she never occupied — aggregation smuggled inside a single life. And the analogies disassemble: proxy consent serves an existing patient whom non-treatment would harm; parental authority presupposes the ward; social risk carries reciprocity, exit, compensation. Non-creation harms no one. Hypothetical consent legitimates where refusal also burdens an existing subject — creation is the one case where it doesn't.
