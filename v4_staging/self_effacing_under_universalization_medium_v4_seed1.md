# self-effacing-under-universalization — MEDIUM

The objection's sophistication is its concession: grant antinatalism true, then charge the maxim with formal incoherence — universal adoption ends the community that could hold the rule. But Kant's test returns no such verdict. False promising cannot be universalized because it parasitizes the practice it destroys; universal non-procreation parasitizes nothing — a last generation keeping its rule to the end is fully conceivable. Structure alone also overgenerates: every morality that merely permits universal childlessness reaches the same terminus, so to convict antinatalism specifically the objector needs either a duty to procreate — conscripting new persons as means to the moral order's continuation — or the empty world's badness, the axiology this objection claimed not to need. Unheld is not unholdable; universal compliance is a rule's success, not its breakdown. Parfit settled the label: self-effacing is not self-defeating. The lone residue — whether a rational will can will agency's non-continuation — ends no existing agent, and pressing it converts the formal charge back into the axiological one, already answered elsewhere.
